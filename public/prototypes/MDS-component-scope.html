Moodle Design System · Scope proposal

What the prototypes proved we're missing

Seven throwaway prototypes were built to explore the book, quiz, forum, assignment, course settings, course index and sticky footer. Every one of them reused the MDS token layer without complaint. Every one of them then hand-built the same twenty-five components, because MDS doesn't ship them yet. This is that list, in the order we should build it.

Read this as evidence, not as a specification

The seven prototypes behind this list are exploratory. They were built to provoke a conversation about direction — they are conceptual, and none of them has been through user testing or design validation. Some of what they propose will change or be dropped.

What they do give us is coverage. Between them they span Moodle's most-used activities — book, quiz, forum and assignment — plus the course page, course settings and the navigation furniture around all of it. A component that four unrelated prototypes each had to build by hand is a real gap regardless of whether that particular prototype's design survives.

So: treat the component list as well-evidenced, and the specimens below as illustrative of shape and behaviour rather than final visual design.

Every specimen below is built from real MDS token values. The page's own left rail is a working instance of §2.1.

What MDS ships today

A strong token layer with a thin component layer. The existing set is well chosen — the prototypes reused all of it.

Tokens cover colour, typography, spacing, borders, radii, shadows, sizes and breakpoints. That layer is why seven independently built pages look like one product. The component layer is where the work is.

The form system

The largest gap, and the one that blocks the most work. Four prototypes — book, quiz, assignment, course settings — are substantially made of forms.

1.1

Field wrapper

Build
Needed by BookQuizAssignmentCourse settings

Binds a label, required marker, help text, error message and the control itself into one unit with consistent spacing. Everything else in this section depends on it. Without a wrapper, every team re-decides where help text sits relative to the label. It's also where the accessibility contract lives — for/id association, aria-describedby for help, aria-invalid for errors.

Conditional field indicator

Build
Needed by Course settings ×22AssignmentQuiz

A marker on a field explaining why it is visible. This is the most Moodle-specific item on the list and the highest-value. Around a third of the course settings form is conditional, which is why two admins comparing screens see genuinely different forms and neither can tell why.

Why now No mainstream design system has this, because no mainstream product has Moodle's configurability. Building it turns a long-standing support burden into an explained behaviour — and it's the one component on this list that no competitor can borrow.

§2

Page architecture

Three structural patterns that every settings and management surface reaches for.

2.1

Two-column anchored settings layout

Build
Needed by BookQuizAssignmentCourse settings

Sticky section rail on the left, form on the right, rail highlights as you scroll. All four settings prototypes independently arrived at this.

Moodle activity and course settings are currently eight to eleven collapsed accordions. You can't see the shape of the form, you can't tell how much is left, and you open sections one at a time hunting for a field. The anchored layout makes a long form navigable without hiding anything.

§T

Token-layer gaps

Cheap to add, and the first one is causing a live bug today.

T1

Layering / z-index scale

Build
Evidenced bySticky footer regression

There are no layering tokens at all. The sticky-footer regression is a layering bug — the footer and the drawers disagree about who sits on top, and the answer currently lives in scattered SCSS across theme_boost. A defined scale would have made that bug structurally impossible.

T2

Motion tokens

Build
Evidenced bySticky footer

No duration or easing tokens, and no reduced-motion guidance. The sticky-footer prototype found that the proposed patch silently drops a prefers-reduced-motion guard — precisely what a token-level answer prevents.

Proposed
--mds-duration-fast · 120ms --mds-duration-base · 200ms --mds-duration-slow · 320ms --mds-ease-standard --mds-ease-enter
T3

Focus ring specification

Extend
Prerequisite for§4.1 row actions

MDS has two focus colour tokens — --mds-focus-default and --mds-focus-danger — but no ring specification: no width, no offset, no style. Given that hover-revealed row actions are central to the course-editing direction, a defined focus treatment is an accessibility prerequisite, not polish.
