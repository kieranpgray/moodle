1
Full width means the whole shell
Full width is a layout mode, not a max-width swap. It takes the same 1440px shell the top navigation gets, and the drawers stop reserving space and float over the content instead — reusing what core already does below the lg breakpoint (drawer.scss:174, “the drawer acts as a modal with a backdrop effect”). Measured here: 828px of grid becomes 1343px. An earlier pass defined full width as the gap between two open drawers, which came to 993px — 163px more than limited, which is not a mode.
2
Paging is not a width
How much room the page gets and how you travel along the columns are separate questions, so they are separate controls. Paged fits as many columns as the space allows and steps a page at a time, nothing scrolling sideways; Scroll puts every selected column on one row, which is what the grader report does today. Measured across the 40 default columns: paged gives 4 a page at 830px and 7 at 1343px; scroll gives 5,114px and 4,599px of horizontal travel respectively.
3
A window, not a scrollbar
The measured grader report on this course is 13,169px wide inside a 784px viewport — 16.8 screens sideways. Paging replaces free horizontal scroll with a window you step through, so “where am I in the gradebook” is a sentence you can read rather than a scrollbar thumb you have to remember. It defaults to as many columns as fit, so it spends whatever width the mode above gave it instead of showing six regardless.
4
Columns wide enough to name
Columns are 140px minimum with a three-line name and nothing else in the header. An earlier pass used 86px and hung a “/100” under every name: six columns a page, every one of them reading “Assign…”, and a whole line of header height spent repeating the same range 61 times. Core does not put the range in the header either — it is a separate row, off by default (grade_report_showranges), and it is reinstated as one, switchable from Preferences. The cost is 4 columns a page at 830px instead of 6; the columns you are not looking at are one click away, not gone.
5
Columns, promoted
Core can already hide a column — through that column’s own … menu, one at a time. Getting from 75 columns down to the handful you are marking is 60-odd menus. Here it is one panel with presets, and the default column set is not “all of them”: it is the items that have a grade or a submission waiting, which on this dataset is 40 of 75.
6
Marking is the job
On the real course, 29 of 427 cells have a grade; on this stress-test dataset it is 83 of 525. Either way the grid spends most of its width showing you the empty ones, undifferentiated. Cells with something waiting are marked in the cell itself rather than behind a filter you have to know to apply — a teacher opening the gradebook is nearly always here to mark something.
7
All thirteen destinations
The option list, its grouping and its order are taken verbatim from the live instance. Five are full-bleed in core (pagelayout report) and get the width control; the other eight are already capped by standard, admin or incourse and do not. Each page says which it is, so the rule is visible rather than folklore.

Visibility dropdown
Today this is reached through the ⋮ menu and reported separately as a badge on the activity. It becomes a single three-state dropdown on the card, showing the current setting at a glance. Fully accessible.

Drag handle
Today the handle shows the four-way move cursor. Here it becomes an open hand that closes as you pick the activity up — the drag-and-drop most people already know from everywhere else. It only appears while you are editing.

The label appears while you are editing, where it helps you tell one activity from another at a glance. It is not shown when you are just viewing the course, and students never see it.

Two-column anchored settings
Ten collapsed accordions become one scrollable form with a sticky section rail that tracks where you are. Nothing is hidden, and you can see how long the form actually is.

Each course format contributes its own settings. Today changing the format needs an Update format button that reloads the page before you can see them. Try switching between Topics, Weekly, Single activity and Social.
