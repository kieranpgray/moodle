1
Status as a journey
Submission status, grading status and time remaining are three rows in a key/value table today. Here they become one track a student can read at a glance — and the marking step only appears when marking workflow is switched on.
2
Manage menu
The assignment's secondary nav — Settings, Advanced grading, Overrides, Download submissions, Reveal identities, Permissions, Logs, Backup — collapses into one educator-only dropdown.
3
Submission shown, not linked
Each enabled submission type renders its own block inline. Today online text and files are squeezed into cells of the same status table.
4
Feedback as a result, not a table
The grade leads, then the marker's comments, the annotated file and the completed rubric. Any late penalty is shown as an adjustment on the grade rather than buried in a row.
5
Counts become filters
The grading summary's numbers are the real ones from view.php. Here each tile is clickable and drops you into the submissions list already filtered — Needs grading is the one teachers actually want.
6
One filter row
The real filters live in three separate places — a status dropdown, an "extra filters" dropdown for workflow and marker, and a separate options form. They become one visible row of chips with live counts.
7
Bulk actions in context
Selecting rows reveals the real batch operations — grant extension, message, lock, set marking state, allocate marker, download. Today they sit in a "With selected…" dropdown at the very bottom of the page.
8
Students in the left drawer
While grading, the course index becomes the class list — searchable, filterable, showing who still needs marking. Today this is an autocomplete dropdown plus two arrows, and you can't see where you are in the cohort.
9
Grade panel kept
The split of submission on the left and grading on the right is the existing grader and it works — it stays. What changes is that Save moves into the same sticky footer the rest of the course uses.
10
One manage workspace
Submissions, Settings, Advanced grading, Overrides and Reports are separate pages today. They become tabs on one surface, so you never lose your place while setting an assignment up.
11
Anchored settings
Every field from the real settings form, in one scrolling page with a sticky section rail that tracks where you are — instead of eleven collapsed accordions you have to open one at a time.
12
Submission types as cards
Turning a type on reveals its own settings inside the card. Today the plugin checkboxes sit in one group and their settings are appended further down the same flat form.
13
Conditional fields, made visible
Marking workflow reveals allocation; allocation reveals marker count; more than one marker reveals the combining method and its rounding. These are the real rules from the settings form — shown as they unfold rather than as greyed-out clutter.
14
Course Linear Navigation
The same sticky Previous/Next footer used across the course. An assignment is a single node, so it steps straight to its neighbours — this already exists in core, it is not new work.
15
Prototype state switcher
Demo-only, not a proposed feature. Flip between student and teacher states, the manage tabs, and the settings variants that change what the screens show.
